A stakeholder asks for a new feature. What problem are they trying to solve? Who else does it affect? How will the team know the result meets the need? This roadmap follows those questions from the first conversation through requirements, process models, and Agile delivery.

<a id="module-1"></a>

## 1. Introduction to Business Analysis

- Define business analysis and explain why teams analyze a need before choosing a solution.
- Compare the responsibilities of a business analyst, systems analyst, and data analyst. Explore how analysts collaborate with pre-sales, the product owner, the project manager, and the product manager.
- Follow the software development life cycle (SDLC). Compare Waterfall and Agile approaches and identify where the analyst contributes in each.
- Identify the skills behind that work, including asking precise questions, facilitating conversations, and communicating decisions.

<a id="module-2"></a>

## 2. Understanding Business Needs

- Define the business need, identify the problem, and investigate its causes.
- Describe the business goal, turn it into measurable objectives, and agree on the scope.
- Identify stakeholder types and plan how to engage them. Use a RACI matrix to distinguish who is **responsible**, **accountable**, **consulted**, and **informed** for a decision.

<a id="module-3"></a>

## 3. Requirements Gathering and Elicitation

- Define a requirement. Distinguish what a solution must do (functional requirements) from qualities or constraints it must satisfy (non-functional requirements).
- Use interviews, surveys, and questionnaires to collect perspectives. Bring stakeholders together for workshops and brainstorming.
- Analyze existing documents and observe people doing the work. Use wireframes and prototypes to surface assumptions and invite feedback.
- Organize and prioritize requirements with MoSCoW or the Kano model. Verify that requirements are clear and consistent, then validate with stakeholders that they address the business need.
- Trace a requirement from its source to the work that implements it and the check that confirms it was met.

<a id="module-4"></a>

## 4. Process Modeling

Use a model to make one aspect of a process clear enough for stakeholders to discuss.

| Model | What to show |
| --- | --- |
| UML use case diagram | The actors interacting with a system and the goals they pursue. |
| UML activity diagram | Steps, decisions, and alternative paths in a workflow. |
| UML sequence diagram | Messages exchanged between participants and the order they occur in. |
| Entity-relationship diagram (ERD) | The data entities a process depends on and how they relate. |
| Wireframe | The screen layout at a level of fidelity suited to the feedback you need. |

<a id="module-5"></a>

## 5. Documenting and Managing Requirements

- Document functional, non-functional, and technical requirements. Write use cases to describe interactions and user stories to express a user's need and its value.
- Build a feature list, a business requirements document (BRD), and a software requirements specification (SRS). Use a change request (CR) to record a proposed revision and its impact.
- Manage requirements in Jira or Trello so the team can follow ownership, status, and changes.

<a id="module-6"></a>

## 6. Tools and Techniques in Business Analysis

| Task | Tools or techniques |
| --- | --- |
| Organize requirements and track work | Jira or Trello |
| Draw and revise process models | draw.io or Microsoft Visio |
| Collaborate on specifications and requirements lists | Google Docs, Google Sheets, or Microsoft Office |
| Assess a business situation | SWOT analysis of strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats |

<a id="module-7"></a>

## 7. Real-World Application and Case Studies

Examine a real business analysis project in detail. Identify the original need, whose input shaped the requirements, and what the team delivered. Compare the outcome with the original objectives. Record a practice worth repeating and a decision you would revisit, with the evidence behind each.

<a id="module-8"></a>

## 8. Agile Methodologies in Business Analysis

- **Agile foundations.** Study the four values and twelve principles. Compare Agile with traditional Waterfall delivery and examine how short feedback cycles help analysts refine requirements.
- **Scrum.** Explore the responsibilities of the Product Owner, Scrum Master, and Developers. Follow work through the Product Backlog, Sprint Backlog, and Increment. Study the Sprint and its events — Sprint Planning, Daily Scrum, Sprint Review, and Sprint Retrospective.
- **Kanban.** Learn its core principles, visualize the workflow, set work-in-progress (WIP) limits, and examine where work accumulates.
- **Other approaches.** Explore Extreme Programming (XP), Lean, and the Scaled Agile Framework (SAFe). Compare these with the V-Model and its links between development and testing.
- **Working with the backlog.** Write user stories with acceptance criteria, refine backlog items, and practice estimation with Planning Poker and T-shirt sizing.
